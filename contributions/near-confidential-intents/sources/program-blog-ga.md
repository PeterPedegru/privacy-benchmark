Confidential Intents brings that property to cross-chain DeFi, where users access markets directly through their own wallets.   The Advantages of Confidential Intents’ Privacy Architecture  Confidential Intents reflects a set of deliberate architecture choices that separate it from other approaches to onchain privacy. The design is covered in depth in  How Confidential Intents Works . The architectural choices that matter most for cross-chain builders and businesses are these:   Encryption over proof generation: a faster, simpler experience for your users.  ZK-based privacy systems require users to generate client-side cryptographic proofs for each transaction, which adds computational overhead, wallet complexity, and limits the set of supported operations. Confidential Intents provides confidentiality through a private-shard execution environment instead. The result is a standard user experience with no client-side proof generation, state sync, or additional wallet setup.   Individual encryption, not pooled obfuscation: your users retain individually encrypted transaction histories rather than participating in a shared anonymity pool.  Mixing services accept funds from any source and pool them to break the link between origin and destination, leaving users to inherit whatever risk comes with the shared pool. Confidential Intents takes the opposite approach. Assets are screened at ingress, and each user's data is encrypted individually on the private shard rather than mixed with others'. Order size, timing, counterparties, and strategy are not publicly visible onchain.   Selective disclosure: compliance without surrendering confidentiality.  As more of the world adopts crypto, more of it falls under regulation, and users increasingly need to share transaction history with counterparties like banks or off-ramp providers. Confidential Intents plans to roll out a selective disclosure feature for exactly this: users hold an account-level view key for their confidential data and can choose to share it with an auditor, tax advisor, compliance officer, or other party for a limited, time-scoped period. Disclosure is user-initiated, while remaining subject to applicable law, regulatory obligations, and NEAR Intents’ compliance and security controls. Combined with screening at ingress, this approach is intended to support the long-term sustainability of Confidential Intents.   How Integration Works  Confidential Intents runs behind the same  1Click Swap API  that partners already use.
